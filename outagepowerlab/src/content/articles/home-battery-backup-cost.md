This guide uses installer marketplace data from EnergySage, updated in 2026, plus a 2025 Tesla Powerwall cost survey from This Old House. All were read on November 27, 2026. We have not installed a home battery. Battery pricing is quoted per job, so your price depends on your house, your installer and your state.

## The short answer

| What you back up | Batteries | Installed cost before incentives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Essential loads (fridge, lights, Wi-Fi, some outlets) | 1 (10 to 13.5 kWh) | $5,000 to $18,000 |
| Whole home | 2 to 3 | $24,000 to $40,000 |
| Typical single 13.5 kWh battery | 1 | about $15,647 on average |

All figures are EnergySage's, based on its marketplace data from the first half of 2026. Its own summary gives a slightly different whole-home range, $22,000 to $40,000, so treat the edges as soft.

## What drives the price

### Brand and price per kWh

EnergySage does not publish one national price per kWh, but it gives brand averages and a range:

- **Tesla:** about $967 per kWh on its cost page, and $998 per kWh for the Powerwall 3 in its review
- **Enphase:** about $1,429 per kWh
- **Budget options:** under $500 per kWh
- **Some premium brands:** more than $3,000 per kWh

Dividing EnergySage's $15,647 average by 13.5 kWh works out to roughly $1,160 per kWh. That is our arithmetic, not a figure EnergySage publishes.

### How much you back up

This is the biggest lever. A battery that only has to carry the fridge, lights, Wi-Fi and a furnace fan can be one unit. Central air, an electric range, electric heat or a well pump can double or triple the battery count, and the cost.

### Whether you have solar

Without solar, a battery is a one-shot reserve that recharges from the grid once power returns. With solar, it refills during the day, so a single battery can carry essentials through a multi-day outage. Adding solar is a much bigger project and is priced separately.

### Electrical work

Most installs include a backup gateway or transfer device, and some homes need a panel upgrade or a separate backup loads panel. Those costs are inside the installed quotes above, but they vary widely from house to house.

## Tax credits in 2026

EnergySage says the federal residential tax credit for solar-plus-battery systems expired in 2025. Older articles, including This Old House's 2025 Powerwall guide, still describe a 30 percent credit, so be careful with any cost guide dated before 2026. State and utility incentives may still apply. Ask your installer which ones they will file, and check them yourself.

## How long the battery lasts in an outage

EnergySage's estimate: a 13.5 kWh battery runs an average home's full load (28.5 kWh a day) for about **11 hours and 25 minutes**, or **one to three days** on essentials alone. This Old House reports an average of 24 to 36 hours for a Powerwall, and longer with solar recharging it on sunny days.

You can run the same math for your own loads with our [power station runtime calculator](/power-station-runtime-calculator/). Enter the battery's capacity in watt-hours (13.5 kWh is 13,500 Wh).

## Battery or generator?

A battery is silent, needs no fuel and switches over instantly, but it runs out. A standby generator keeps going as long as it has fuel and usually costs less for whole-home coverage. Compare [what a whole-house generator costs](/how-much-does-a-whole-house-generator-cost/) before you decide.
