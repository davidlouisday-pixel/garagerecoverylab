This guide pulls together four published cost guides, read on November 27, 2026. We have not bought or installed a generator. The sources disagree, sometimes a lot, so we show each one rather than inventing an average.

## The short answer

| Source | Typical installed cost | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HomeGuide | $6,000 to $11,000 | $9,500 to $24,000 for long runs or units over 20 kW |
| ConsumerAffairs (2026) | $10,000 to $25,000 | Depends on size, fuel and installation |
| HomeAdvisor | $5,000 to $25,000 for standby units | Its project average is $5,174, which includes smaller jobs |
| Angi | $5,000 to $25,000 for standby units | Same $5,172 average and $1,538 to $8,815 range |

For a typical house getting a 14 to 22 kW air-cooled standby unit on natural gas, plan on roughly **$8,000 to $14,000** all in. That is our reading of where the four sources overlap, not a number any of them publishes.

## Cost by generator size

These are unit prices before installation. HomeGuide says so explicitly. HomeAdvisor's and Angi's tables match each other almost exactly but do not say clearly whether they include labor, so treat them as unit prices too.

| Size | HomeGuide | HomeAdvisor and Angi |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 7 to 10 kW | $2,000 to $3,000 | $2,000 to $3,500 |
| 13 to 18 kW | $3,000 to $4,500 (13 to 16 kW) | $4,000 to $5,500 (14 to 18 kW) |
| 17 to 24 kW | $4,000 to $6,000 (17 to 20 kW) | $5,000 to $6,500 (20 to 24 kW) |
| 22 to 32 kW | $4,500 to $12,000 (22 to 25 kW) | $6,500 to $15,000 (26 to 32 kW) |
| 30 kW and up | $10,000 to $16,000 (30 to 48 kW) | $14,000 to $16,000 (36 to 38 kW) |

Not sure where you land? Our [generator size calculator](/generator-size-calculator/) adds up running and starting watts for the things you want on during an outage.

## What installation includes

The box is often half the bill or less. Here is what the rest pays for.

### Labor

HomeGuide estimates installation at **$3,000 to $5,000**, or **$5,000 to $12,000 and up** for long distances or units over 20 kW. HomeAdvisor, Angi and ConsumerAffairs all put installer labor at **$1,500 to $5,000**. Angi gives an average standby installation of $4,520, including permits, assembly, placement and wiring.

### Transfer switch

Every standby generator needs an automatic transfer switch, which disconnects your house from the grid and switches to the generator when the power fails. Many units include one.

- HomeGuide: **$600 to $2,500** installed for an automatic transfer switch
- HomeAdvisor: **$600 to $2,500**
- ConsumerAffairs: **$500 to $2,000**

Our [transfer switch cost guide](/generator-transfer-switch-cost/) breaks this down by type and amperage.

### Concrete or composite pad

- HomeGuide: **$150 to $400** for a 3 to 4 inch precast pad
- HomeAdvisor and Angi: **$50 to $75 per square foot**, or about **$1,000** for a poured 3 by 6 foot pad

### Fuel line

- Natural gas line: **$12 to $25 per linear foot** (HomeGuide). ConsumerAffairs quotes an installer at **$500 to $2,000** for a typical gas line run.
- Propane tank, if you do not have gas service: **$400 to $1,800** (HomeGuide) or **$300 to $1,500** (HomeAdvisor), plus the propane itself.

### Electrical work and permits

- New circuit: **$570 to $1,000** (Angi)
- Subpanel: **$500 to $2,000** (HomeAdvisor, Angi)
- Panel upgrade, if your panel cannot take it: **$1,000 to $2,200** (ConsumerAffairs)
- Permits: **$50 to $500** depending on the source and your town

## What it costs to run

Fuel is the big variable. HomeGuide's per-day estimates for running a generator continuously:

| Fuel | 7 kW | 15 kW | 20 kW |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Natural gas | $10 to $20 | $22 to $32 | $30 to $40 |
| Liquid propane | $60 to $70 | $145 to $165 | $150 to $170 |
| Diesel | $32 to $40 | $90 to $110 | $100 to $120 |

HomeAdvisor's averages are higher for natural gas ($90 a day) and propane ($220 a day), without stating a size. Either way, the pattern holds: **natural gas is far cheaper to run than propane**, and a multi-day outage on propane costs real money.

Maintenance adds roughly **$150 to $650 a year** for a service contract, depending on the source. Generators also run a short weekly self-test, which burns a little fuel.

## How long a standby generator lasts

Estimates range widely: 15 to 30 years (HomeGuide), 15 to 25 years (ConsumerAffairs), up to 20 years (Angi). HomeAdvisor notes most standby units are built for about 3,000 hours of running and advises against more than 500 continuous hours. In practice, how often you lose power and how well it is maintained matter more than the calendar.

## How to keep the price down

1. **Back up essentials, not the whole house.** A smaller unit with a load-management switch can cost thousands less than a unit sized for central air and an electric range running at once.
2. **Use natural gas if you have it.** It avoids a propane tank and is far cheaper to run.
3. **Put it close to the gas meter and panel.** Long runs of gas line and wire are where installs balloon.
4. **Get two or three quotes** with the same scope in writing: unit, transfer switch, pad, gas, electrical, permit and startup.
5. **Consider the alternatives.** A portable generator plus a transfer switch or interlock kit covers essentials for a fraction of the price, as long as you are home to run it.
