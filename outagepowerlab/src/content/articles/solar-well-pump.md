Water is one of the first problems I hit researching an off-grid cabin: a standard 240V well pump is a big load to run from batteries. This guide pulls together a 2026 cost guide from Angi and solar pumping guides from Virginia Tech, New Mexico State University and NYSERDA, read on November 27, 2026. I have not installed a solar pump. Well work is a job for a licensed well contractor.

## How a solar well pump works

A solar well pump is usually a DC pump wired to its own solar panels through a pump controller. It runs when the sun is out and pumps faster when the sun is stronger. Instead of storing energy in batteries, you store **water**: the pump fills a tank or an uphill reservoir during the day, and you draw from the tank whenever you need it.

Virginia Tech's extension guide notes that storing already-pumped water avoids the "operation and maintenance costs associated with battery banks". The trade-off is that you need a tank big enough to cover cloudy days.

## Step 1: work out daily water use

Published planning figures:

- **USGS:** "each person uses about 80-100 gallons of water per day, for indoor home uses."
- **EPA WaterSense:** "The average American family uses more than 300 gallons of water per day at home," roughly 70 percent of it indoors.
- **NYSERDA's pumping guide:** 50 gallons per person per day for household planning.

A frugal off-grid cabin can use far less than a suburban house, but start with a real estimate. Two people at NYSERDA's 50 gallons each is 100 gallons a day.

## Step 2: size the storage tank

**Tank size = daily water use x 3**

Virginia Tech, New Mexico State and NYSERDA all use about three days of storage as the rule of thumb. New Mexico State notes it "may be up to ten" depending on conditions. At 100 gallons a day, that is a 300 gallon tank at minimum.

## Step 3: size the pump's flow rate

**Flow (gallons per minute) = daily gallons / peak sun hours / 60**

The pump has to deliver a whole day's water while the sun is up. Virginia Tech and NYSERDA both size flow by dividing daily gallons by peak sun hours. At 100 gallons a day and 4 peak sun hours, that is 25 gallons an hour, or about 0.4 gallons a minute. Get peak sun hours for your site from NREL's free PVWatts tool.

## Step 4: know your total dynamic head

Total dynamic head (TDH) is how hard the pump has to push. NYSERDA defines it as "the sum of the static lift of the water, the static height of the storage tank, and the losses from friction." A deep well, a tank on a hill and a long pipe run all raise it, and the pump curve tells you what flow you get at that head. This is the number your well contractor or pump supplier will ask for.

## Step 5: size the panels

The pump maker specifies panel wattage for a given head and flow. Then add margin:

- NYSERDA: "increase the specified wattage by 25% (multiply by 1.25)," to cover high heat, dust and aging.
- Virginia Tech also recommends oversizing panels by about 125 percent.
- New Mexico State's design spreadsheet uses only 85 percent of a panel's rated power.

## What it costs

Angi's 2026 figures:

| Well depth | Installed cost |
| --- | --- |
| Up to 150 feet | $1,200 to $2,000 |
| 150 to 300 feet | $2,500 to $3,000 |
| 300 to 400+ feet | $3,500 to $10,000 |

- **National average:** about $2,000.
- **Pump only, by flow:** about $950 at 1.5 gallons a minute, $1,100 at 3.5 and $1,500 at 10.
- **Example kits:** a 200 W pump with two panels rated to 150 feet and 5.1 gallons a minute at $2,150; an 800 W pump with eight panels rated to 325 feet and 8.9 gallons a minute at $3,500.
- **Panels:** Angi says many pump kits do not include panels, which it prices at $1 to $1.50 per watt for the common 100 W size.

## Limits to plan around

- **Night and cloudy days.** No sun, no pumping. That is what the tank is for.
- **Pressure.** Pumping into a tank gives you stored water, not house pressure. Most setups add a small pressure pump or use gravity from an uphill tank.
- **Freezing.** Virginia Tech's guide is written for livestock watering in "freeze-free months". In cold climates, protect the tank and pipes.
