Price was the first wall I hit researching power for an off-grid cabin: the numbers online ranged from a few thousand dollars to six figures. This guide explains why, using HomeGuide's off-grid solar cost guide and EnergySage's battery cost data, both read on November 27, 2026. HomeGuide's page shows no date, so treat its figures as a recent snapshot. I have not bought a system yet.

## The short answer

| System | Installed cost (HomeGuide) |
| --- | --- |
| DC solar only (no backup) | $6,500 to $11,000 |
| Mixed DC and AC | $11,000 to $60,000 |
| AC solar only | $32,000 to $70,000 |
| AC solar with backup generator | $42,000 to $80,000 |
| Typical whole home, 5 to 10 kW | $40,000 to $80,000, about $60,000 most often |

For comparison, HomeGuide puts a 5 to 10 kW **grid-tied** system at $15,000 to $45,000. Going off-grid roughly doubles the price, mostly because of batteries.

## Cost by system size

HomeGuide's installed totals:

| Size | Installed cost |
| --- | --- |
| 3 kW | $30,000 to $35,000 |
| 4 kW | $35,000 to $40,000 |
| 5 kW | $40,000 to $45,000 |
| 10 kW | $70,000 to $80,000 |
| 15 kW | $85,000 to $100,000 |
| 20 kW | $100,000 to $120,000 |

Most cabins land far below a 5 kW home system. Our [cabin sizing guide](/off-grid-cabin-solar-system/) works through a weekend cabin needing about 300 W of panels and a full-time cabin needing about 1,300 W.

## Where the money goes

HomeGuide's component prices:

- **Batteries: $17,500 to $40,000 or more.** The biggest line by far. EnergySage puts a typical 13.5 kWh home battery at about $15,647 installed before incentives, and says covering off-grid needs can run "over $115,000".
- **Solar panels: $0.80 to $1.80 per watt** installed, before incentives.
- **Inverter: $1,500 to $3,000 or more.**
- **Charge controllers: $100 to $1,000.**
- **Backup generator: $6,000 to $11,000** for a whole-house unit, optional.
- **Labor: 7 to 10 percent** of the total, about $3,000 to $8,000 for 5 to 10 kW.

## Why a cabin can cost a fraction of a house

The battery bank scales with daily energy use, and daily use scales with what you choose to run. Three choices shrink an off-grid system more than any discount:

1. **Run DC where you can.** HomeGuide's cheapest category, DC-only at $6,500 to $11,000, covers lights and DC appliances without a large inverter.
2. **Pick an efficient fridge.** A standard fridge can be a third of a cabin's daily load. A DC fridge rated at 150 Wh a day uses about one eighth as much.
3. **Heat and cook with something other than electricity.** Wood and propane keep the battery bank small.

Use the [off-grid solar calculator](/off-grid-solar-calculator/) to see how each change moves the panel and battery size.

## Do you need a generator too?

It depends on how many cloudy days your battery must cover. A generator lets you buy fewer days of battery, which is the expensive part. HomeGuide's AC-with-generator category is $42,000 to $80,000 for a whole home. A smaller portable generator for a cabin costs much less; see [what size generator you need](/what-size-generator-do-i-need/).

## Permits and installers

HomeGuide advises hiring only "a licensed, bonded, and insured solar contractor" and asking whether the installation requires a permit. Rules for remote cabins vary widely by county, so check with your local building department before you buy equipment.
