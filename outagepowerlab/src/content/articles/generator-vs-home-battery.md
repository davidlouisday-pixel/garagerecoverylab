## Start with how long your outages last

EIA reports that U.S. electricity customers averaged about **5.6 hours** of power interruptions in 2022, including major events, and about two hours a year when major events are excluded. For most people, most years, that is well within one battery's reach.

The catch is the rare long one: an ice storm or hurricane that leaves you dark for three days. A battery without solar cannot ride that out at full load. A generator can, as long as the gas keeps flowing or the propane tank lasts.

So the honest question is not "which is better" but **"how long is the worst outage I need to survive, and what must stay on?"**

## When a generator makes more sense

- You lose power for **days** at a time, not hours.
- You want to run **central air, electric heat or a well pump** through an outage.
- You have **natural gas** at the house, which makes running costs low.
- You want **whole-home coverage at the lowest price**.

## When a battery makes more sense

- Your outages are usually **short**, a few hours.
- You need **silence or no exhaust**, for example on a tight lot or under HOA rules.
- You have or plan **solar**, which lets a battery recharge every day.
- You care most about **essentials**: fridge, lights, Wi-Fi, medical devices.

## The cheapest option of all

If you only need essentials and you are home during outages, a portable generator with a transfer switch or interlock kit, or a portable power station for small loads, costs a fraction of either system. See [transfer switch costs](/generator-transfer-switch-cost/) and the [power station runtime calculator](/power-station-runtime-calculator/).

## Safety is not equal

Fuel-burning generators produce carbon monoxide. The CPSC says an average of nearly 100 people die each year in the U.S. from CO poisoning from portable generators. Standby generators are installed outside to code, but portable ones are only as safe as where you put them: never in a garage, and more than 20 feet from windows, doors and vents. Read [why the garage is never safe](/can-you-run-a-generator-in-a-garage/). Batteries avoid that risk entirely.
