This guide uses Honda's published appliance wattage estimates and The Home Depot's generator sizing guide, read on November 27, 2026. Your appliances may draw more or less than these averages, so check their labels where you can. For a standby generator, a licensed electrician should do a proper load calculation before you buy.

Want the quick version? Our [generator size calculator](/generator-size-calculator/) does this math for you.

## The three-step method

1. **Add the running watts** of everything you want on at the same time.
2. **Add the largest starting surge.** Find the appliance with the biggest gap between its starting and running watts, and add that gap. Motors start one at a time, so you only need headroom for the biggest one.
3. **Divide by 0.9.** The Home Depot advises running a generator at no more than 90 percent of capacity.

The result is the rated (running) output to look for on the spec sheet, not the peak or "starting" number brands often print largest.

## Common appliance wattages

Honda's estimates, in watts:

| Appliance | Running | Starting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Refrigerator or freezer (Energy Star) | 132 to 192 | 1,200 |
| Furnace fan, 1/3 HP | 700 | 1,400 |
| Furnace fan, 1/2 HP | 875 | 2,350 |
| Sump pump, 1/3 HP | 800 | 1,300 |
| Sump pump, 1/2 HP | 1,050 | 2,150 |
| Window air conditioner, 10,000 BTU | 1,500 | 2,200 |
| Microwave, 1,000 W | 1,500 | 1,500 |
| Coffee maker, 4 cup | 600 | 600 |
| Washing machine | 1,200 | 1,200 |
| Electric clothes dryer | 5,400 | 6,750 |
| Electric water heater | 4,500 | 4,500 |
| Electric range, one 8-inch element | 2,100 | 2,100 |
| Garage door opener | 720 | 1,420 |
| Flat screen TV, 46 inch | 190 | 190 |
| Laptop | 200 to 250 | 200 to 250 |

Some big loads are missing for a reason. Central air conditioners and well pumps vary too much to estimate from a table. The Home Depot lists central air at 2,000 to 4,000 running watts and a water pump at 1,000 to 2,000, without starting figures. For those, read the nameplate or ask an electrician.

## Example 1: essentials only, about 4,000 watts

Fridge, 1/3 HP furnace fan, 1/2 HP sump pump, eight 10 W LED bulbs, a TV and a laptop.

- Running: 192 + 700 + 1,050 + 80 + 190 + 250 = **2,462 W**
- Largest surge: the sump pump, 2,150 minus 1,050 = **1,100 W**
- Peak: 2,462 + 1,100 = 3,562 W
- Divided by 0.9: **about 4,000 W**

A 4,000 to 5,000 watt portable generator covers this, connected through a transfer switch or interlock kit, or with extension cords to each appliance.

## Example 2: essentials plus comfort, about 8,000 watts

Everything in example 1, plus a 10,000 BTU window AC, a 1,000 W microwave and a coffee maker.

- Running: 2,462 + 1,500 + 1,500 + 600 = **6,062 W**
- Largest surge: still the sump pump, **1,100 W**
- Peak: 7,162 W
- Divided by 0.9: **about 8,000 W**

That is the top of the portable range. You can get by with less if you avoid running the microwave and the AC at the same time.

## Example 3: electric appliances, about 22,000 watts

Everything in example 2, plus an electric water heater, an electric dryer and one range element.

- Running: 6,062 + 4,500 + 5,400 + 2,100 = **18,062 W**
- Largest surge: the dryer, 6,750 minus 5,400 = **1,350 W**
- Peak: 19,412 W
- Divided by 0.9: **about 22,000 W**

This is whole-house standby territory. See [what a whole-house generator costs](/how-much-does-a-whole-house-generator-cost/) by size.

## A rougher rule you will see

The Home Depot's guide also offers a quick formula for a motor appliance: running watts plus three times running watts. For a small fridge running at 350 W, that is 350 + 1,050 = 1,400 W. It is a reasonable rule of thumb for a single motor, but it oversizes a whole-house list if you apply it to everything.

## Size, then connect it safely

- **240V loads** such as well pumps, dryers, water heaters and ranges need a generator with 240V output and a transfer switch or interlock that feeds both sides of your panel. See [transfer switch costs](/generator-transfer-switch-cost/).
- **Never run the generator in a garage** or near open windows. [What the CPSC and CDC say](/can-you-run-a-generator-in-a-garage/).
