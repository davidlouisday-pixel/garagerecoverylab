This guide shows the runtime math with published appliance figures, read on November 27, 2026. We have not measured these fridges or power stations ourselves. Real runtime changes with your fridge's age, the room temperature and how often the door opens, so treat these as planning estimates.

## The formula

**Runtime (hours) = usable battery energy (Wh) / fridge's average draw (W)**

- **Usable battery energy:** the station's capacity times inverter efficiency. We assume 85 percent, a common planning figure, not a measured number for any one product. Check your manual.
- **Fridge's average draw:** a fridge compressor cycles on and off, so it does not draw its running watts all the time. Take the estimated yearly energy use in kWh from the yellow EnergyGuide label, or look the model up in the ENERGY STAR product finder, and divide it by 8.76.

Example: a fridge rated 450 kWh a year averages 450 / 8.76 = **about 51 watts**. A 1,000 Wh station at 85 percent gives 850 Wh, and 850 / 51 = **about 16.5 hours**.

## Runtime by power station size

At 85 percent inverter efficiency:

| Station capacity | Efficient fridge (350 kWh/yr, ~40 W) | Typical fridge (450 kWh/yr, ~51 W) | Larger or older fridge (650 kWh/yr, ~74 W) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 500 Wh | about 11 hours | about 8 hours | about 6 hours |
| 1,000 Wh | about 21 hours | about 16 hours | about 11 hours |
| 2,000 Wh | about 42 hours | about 33 hours | about 23 hours |
| 3,000 Wh | about 64 hours | about 50 hours | about 34 hours |
| 5,000 Wh | about 106 hours | about 83 hours | about 57 hours |

The three fridge sizes are examples to show the range, not measurements of specific models. Plug your own label number into our [power station runtime calculator](/power-station-runtime-calculator/), along with anything else you want to run.

## Make sure it can start the fridge

Capacity is how long it lasts. Output is whether it works at all. Honda estimates an Energy Star fridge at **132 to 192 running watts** but **about 1,200 watts to start** the compressor. Check two numbers in the station's specs:

- **Continuous output** above the fridge's running watts plus anything else you run at the same time.
- **Surge (peak) output** above the fridge's starting watts.

## How to stretch the runtime

1. **Keep the doors shut.** FoodSafety.gov says a fridge keeps food safe for up to 4 hours with no power at all, and a full freezer about 48 hours (24 if half full and kept closed).
2. **Turn the temperature to the warm end of safe.** Every degree colder costs energy.
3. **Use the station's DC or USB ports** for phones and lights, so they skip the inverter's losses.
4. **Recharge with solar** if your station accepts panels.

## When a power station is not enough

If you need the fridge plus a sump pump, furnace fan and lights for days, a generator or home battery is the better tool. See [what size generator you need](/what-size-generator-do-i-need/) and [what a home battery costs](/home-battery-backup-cost/).
