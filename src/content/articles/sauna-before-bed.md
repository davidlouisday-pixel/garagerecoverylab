This guide is based on the sources listed at the end, plus Cole's own routine, labeled as such. Most of the research here is about warm baths, not saunas, and we say so where it matters.

## The short answer

| Question | What the evidence says |
| --- | --- |
| Does a sauna improve sleep? | Plausible, but there are no solid sauna-specific trials in healthy adults that we could find |
| What is the best indirect evidence? | A 2019 meta-analysis of warm baths and showers before bed |
| How long before bed? | 1 to 2 hours, about 90 minutes in the researchers' summary |
| What can backfire? | Going to bed hot, dehydrated, or after alcohol |

## The best evidence: warm baths before bed

In 2019, researchers at the University of Texas at Austin, led by Shahab Haghayegh, published a systematic review and meta-analysis in *Sleep Medicine Reviews*. They screened **5,322 studies**, included **17**, and pooled **13** with comparable data.

According to the university summaries of the paper:

- Water at about **104 to 109°F (40 to 42.5°C)** improved **self-rated sleep quality** and **sleep efficiency**.
- Bathing **1 to 2 hours before bed** helped people fall asleep **about 10 minutes faster**.
- The best timing was about **90 minutes** before bed.

The authors also said more research is needed on the best timing, duration and exact mechanism.

### Why warming up helps

Your core body temperature falls in the evening as part of your daily body clock, and it is lowest while you sleep. Warming the body draws blood to the hands and feet, which then give off heat and speed up that fall. That is the mechanism the Texas team describes.

The flip side matters. A 2012 review by Okamoto-Mizuno and Mizuno in the *Journal of Physiological Anthropology* found that heat exposure lasting **through the night**, such as a hot bedroom, goes with more time awake and less deep sleep. The goal is to warm up and then cool down before bed, not to go to bed hot.

## What about saunas specifically?

The direct evidence is weak, and we would rather say that than overstate it:

- **Surveys.** You may see "83% of people sleep better after a sauna." That figure comes from a survey of how people perceive their sleep, not from measured sleep.
- **Wearable data.** The ring maker Ultrahuman analyzed **180** of its users and found sleep efficiency about **0.9 percentage points** higher on sauna nights. The company called this observational, not causal, and estimated deep sleep was slightly lower.
- **Trials.** We could not find a published randomized trial of sauna use and sleep in healthy adults.

A sauna is also a bigger heat load than a bath. In research reported by the University of Jyväskylä in 2026, heart rate in **50 adults** rose from about **79 to 121 beats per minute** over a 10-minute sauna session, and more humid air raised the load further. That is another reason to leave time to settle before bed.

## How to try it

If you want to test an evening sauna, this routine follows the warm-bath research. It is our suggestion, not a tested sauna protocol:

1. **Finish 1 to 2 hours before bed.** Aim for about 90 minutes.
2. **Keep the session moderate.** 15 to 20 minutes is plenty. See [how long to stay in a sauna](/how-long-should-you-stay-in-a-sauna/).
3. **Cool down afterwards** with a lukewarm or cool shower, so you are not still flushed at bedtime.
4. **Drink water** to replace what you sweated out, without overdoing it right before bed.
5. **Skip alcohol.** The 2018 Mayo Clinic Proceedings sauna review warns that alcohol plus sauna raises the risk of low blood pressure and falls.
6. **Keep the bedroom cool** so your temperature can keep falling overnight.

Note how long it takes you to fall asleep for a couple of weeks with and without the sauna. That will tell you more than any average.

## Cole's experience

Cole uses his gym's sauna **four or more times a week**, for about **20 minutes at 180 to 190°F**, right after training rather than at night. He says he **falls asleep more easily** on those days. That is one person's experience, and training itself affects sleep, so it does not show what the sauna alone does.
