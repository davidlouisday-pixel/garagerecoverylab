Cole trained in a home gym for years before moving to a commercial gym, and still trains seven days a week. This guide covers how to plan a garage gym. It is not a product review, and it does not recommend specific brands.

## Start with what the training needs

The CDC's guidance for adults is a useful target for what a home gym should make possible:

- **At least 150 minutes a week** of moderate aerobic activity, or 75 minutes of vigorous activity
- **At least 2 days a week** of muscle-strengthening work that hits all the major muscle groups: **legs, hips, back, abdomen, chest, shoulders and arms**

A barbell, rack and bench cover the strength side on their own. Squats and deadlifts train legs, hips and back. Bench and overhead presses train chest, shoulders and arms. Rows and chin-ups cover the upper back. Everything else in a garage gym is a convenience or an extra.

## Idea 1: the one-wall setup

This is the most common garage gym, and it fits beside a parked car in many garages.

- **Rack against the wall**, with the bar facing into the room.
- **Plate storage** on the rack itself or on a wall rack, so plates stay off the floor.
- **Bench** that rolls or slides under the rack when you are not using it.
- **Rubber flooring** under the whole area, plus clear space on both sides of the bar for loading plates.

If you share the garage with a car, a folding wall-mounted rack is the space-saving version of the same idea.

## Idea 2: the full-bay gym

If you can give up a whole bay, you can add:

- A **lifting platform** for deadlifts, so heavy pulls are not landing on thin mats.
- A **cardio machine**, such as a rower, bike or treadmill, to cover the aerobic minutes on cold or rainy days.
- **Dumbbells** on a rack for single-arm work and accessories.
- A **pull-up bar or rings** if the rack does not have one built in.

## Idea 3: gym plus recovery corner

This is the setup this site is built around. A garage often has the space, a concrete floor and an electrical panel nearby, which makes it a practical place for a sauna or cold plunge next to the training area.

- A **two-person sauna** needs a footprint of about 4 by 6 feet. Most traditional heaters need a **dedicated 240V circuit**, while many infrared units plug into a standard outlet. See [infrared vs traditional sauna](/infrared-vs-traditional-sauna/), and size a heater with our [sauna heater size calculator](/sauna-heater-size-calculator/).
- A **cold plunge** needs a level spot, an outlet for any chiller, and somewhere water can drain. Our [best cold plunge](/best-cold-plunge/) guide covers chiller vs ice options.
- Keep the recovery corner **away from the lifting area**, so plates and bars are never swinging near hot heaters or water.

For timing, our guides on [sauna before or after a workout](/sauna-before-or-after-workout/) and [sauna for muscle recovery](/sauna-for-muscle-recovery/) cover what the research says.

## Flooring

Bare concrete is hard on equipment and on dropped weights, and it is cold underfoot in winter.

- **Rubber mats or horse stall mats** across the training area are the usual answer. They protect the slab, quiet the noise and give better grip.
- **Thicker mats or a platform** where you deadlift or drop weights.
- **Keep flooring away from any water**, such as a cold plunge, or use a drain mat there.

## Temperature and airflow

Garages get cold in winter and hot in summer. A few things make training in one easier:

- **A fan and an open door** go a long way in summer.
- **Seal and insulate** the garage door if you train there year-round.
- **Watch for rust** on bars and plates in a humid garage. Wipe chalk and sweat off after sessions.

## What to buy first

1. **Flooring.** Protects everything else.
2. **Rack, barbell and plates.** The core of the gym.
3. **Adjustable bench.** Adds pressing and rows.
4. **Storage.** Plates and bars off the floor.
5. **Conditioning.** A rower or bike for the aerobic minutes.
6. **Recovery gear.** A sauna or cold plunge, once training is a habit.

Buying in that order means every step makes the gym more useful, and you never own a sauna before you own a barbell.
