This guide is based on the medical sources and reviews listed at the end. Cole uses a dry sauna with a rock heater at his gym, and does not use steam rooms regularly, so there is no first-hand steam section here.

## The short answer

| | Sauna (dry) | Steam room |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Air temperature | 150 to 195°F (Cleveland Clinic) | About 110 to 120°F (WebMD), about 122°F (Columbia) |
| Humidity | Under 20% | Close to 100% |
| Heat source | Electric or wood stove with stones | Steam generator |
| Long-term research | Large Finnish studies over 20+ years | Very little |
| Typical session | 15 to 20 minutes | 5 to 10 minutes |

## Why they feel so different

A sauna heats the air to a high temperature but keeps it dry. Your sweat evaporates, which cools your skin, so you can tolerate air that is far hotter than your body.

A steam room fills the air with water vapor. At close to 100% humidity, sweat cannot evaporate, so you lose that cooling effect. That is why 115°F of steam can feel as intense as 180°F of dry heat. In a sauna, pouring water on the stones briefly does something similar: the humidity spikes and the room suddenly feels much hotter.

## What the research actually covers

**Saunas.** The best-known research comes from the Kuopio Ischemic Heart Disease study in eastern Finland, which followed **2,315 middle-aged men** for about **20 years**. Men who used a sauna 4 to 7 times a week had about a **63% lower risk of sudden cardiac death** than men who went once a week. Later papers from the same group linked frequent sauna use with lower rates of dementia, stroke and high blood pressure. These saunas were traditional Finnish saunas, averaging about **174°F** with low humidity. We cover these studies in detail in [how hot should a sauna be](/how-hot-should-a-sauna-be/).

Those are observational studies, so they show an association, not proof that the sauna caused the benefit.

**Steam rooms.** We could not find long-term studies of steam rooms comparable to the Finnish sauna research. A 2018 systematic review of dry sauna studies, which pulled together 40 clinical studies, specifically excluded steam and wet saunas. Health sites list benefits such as clearing congestion, skin health and relaxation, but WebMD's summary of steam room benefits does not cite study figures for them.

That does not mean steam rooms do nothing. It means the strong evidence people quote about "sauna benefits" comes from dry saunas, and should not be assumed to transfer to steam.

## Hygiene

A steam room's warm, wet air is good for bacteria as well as for you. WebMD notes that the warmth and moisture can help bacteria grow, and advises against using a public steam room when you have a skin infection or are sick. Columbia's health service suggests sitting on a towel and wearing shower shoes in shared rooms. A dry sauna has the same shared-surface issue, but much less standing moisture.

## For a home or garage build

This is where the choice is usually made for you.

- **A sauna** is a dry, insulated wooden room with a heater. Infrared models often plug into a normal outlet, and traditional heaters usually need a dedicated 240V circuit. Our [infrared vs traditional sauna](/infrared-vs-traditional-sauna/) guide covers the wiring.
- **A steam room** has to hold saturated air without damaging the building, so it needs a sealed, waterproof enclosure, a steam generator, and a drain. That is closer to building a tiled shower than a sauna.

For most garages, a sauna is the realistic option. If you want a burst of humid heat, a traditional sauna gives you that whenever you ladle water on the stones.

## Who should be careful with either

The same cautions apply to both. Cleveland Clinic advises avoiding saunas after a recent heart attack or stroke and after drinking alcohol, and checking with a doctor first if you are pregnant, over 65, have a heart condition or high or low blood pressure, epilepsy, or take regular medication. WebMD gives similar advice for steam rooms, including low blood pressure, heart disease, a past stroke and heart rhythm problems. Drink water before and after either one.
