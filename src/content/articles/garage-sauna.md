This guide is based on heater manufacturers' sizing and construction guidance and published cost data, listed at the end. Cole trained in a home gym for years before moving to a commercial gym, and now uses a sauna at his gym. He has not built a garage sauna, so this is a planning guide, not a build log.

## The short answer

| Decision | Typical choice |
| --- | --- |
| Footprint | About 4 x 6 ft for 2 people, 6 x 6 ft or more for 3 to 4 |
| Ceiling | About 7 ft. HUUM gives 2.1 to 2.3 m (about 6 ft 11 in to 7 ft 7 in) as normal. |
| Heater | About 1 kW per 35 cubic feet, usually 4.5 to 8 kW |
| Electrical | Dedicated 240V circuit for most traditional heaters, or a 120V outlet for many infrared units |
| Insulation | 2 to 4 inches in walls and ceiling, plus a foil vapor barrier |
| Budget | About $3,000 to $10,000 installed, per HomeGuide |

## Option 1: a kit or prefab sauna in the garage

The easiest route is a prefab indoor sauna or a pre-cut kit, set up on the garage floor. The walls, benches and door come as a package, and many infrared units plug in.

- **Indoor prefab room, installed:** $2,300 to $10,500 (HomeGuide)
- **Pre-cut kit, installed:** $4,300 to $11,500 (HomeGuide)
- **Professional assembly:** $300 to $1,500 for a prefab kit and $1,500 to $2,500 for a pre-cut kit (HomeGuide, Angi)

## Option 2: build a sauna room

If you are framing your own room in a corner of the garage, the heater makers' guidance gives clear targets.

### Size and ceiling

Keep the room as small as your group needs. Every extra cubic foot is more air to heat. HUUM gives a normal ceiling height of **2.1 to 2.3 meters**, about 6 ft 11 in to 7 ft 7 in, with **2 meters** (about 6 ft 7 in) as a minimum. A lower ceiling means less volume to heat.

### Insulation and vapor barrier

HUUM's construction guidance for an efficient sauna:

- **Insulation:** at least **50 mm** (about 2 in) in the walls and ceiling, with **100 mm** (about 4 in) recommended
- **Vapor barrier:** foil, with about a **10 mm** air gap behind the paneling
- **Paneling:** interior boards about **12 to 16 mm** thick

A garage wall is often uninsulated, so a sauna built against it without this layer will struggle to heat and can push moisture into the structure.

### Watch the concrete

Heater makers treat concrete, stone, brick, tile and glass as "cold" surfaces that need extra heater power. Harvia adds **1.2 cubic meters of volume for every square meter** of them, about 4 cubic feet for every square foot. If you leave a concrete wall exposed inside the sauna, size the heater up, or better, cover it with insulation and paneling. Many builders also put wooden duckboards over a concrete floor for comfort.

## Heater size

The heater makers' rule is about **1 kW per cubic meter**, which is about 1 kW per 35 cubic feet.

| Room (L x W x H) | Volume | Minimum power | Common heater size |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 x 6 x 7 ft | 168 cu ft | 4.8 kW | 6 kW |
| 5 x 6 x 7 ft | 210 cu ft | 5.9 kW | 6 kW |
| 6 x 6 x 7 ft | 252 cu ft | 7.1 kW | 8 kW |
| 6 x 8 x 7 ft | 336 cu ft | 9.5 kW | 10.5 kW |

These assume fully insulated walls. Enter your own room, including any glass or concrete, in our [sauna heater size calculator](/sauna-heater-size-calculator/). Harvia also advises picking a heater whose stated room range has your room near the middle, not at the edge.

## Electrical

Most traditional heaters need a **dedicated 240V circuit**. A 6 kW heater on 240V draws 25 amps. As one example, the Almost Heaven Watoga's 6 kW Harvia heater is listed as 240V, 30 amp hardwired. A garage usually has the advantage of being close to the panel, which keeps the wire run short.

- **Cost:** a dedicated 240V circuit runs about **$250 to $900** (HomeGuide) or **$500 to $800** (Fixr).
- **Who does it:** a licensed electrician, following the heater's installation manual. One retailer's instructions for Dundalk barrel saunas specifically say the heater should **not** be on a GFI breaker, which shows why the manual matters.
- **Permit:** a new circuit typically needs one. Check with your town.

Many infrared saunas plug into a standard 120V outlet, which avoids this step.

## Running cost

A garage sauna that is well insulated preheats faster and costs less per session. HomeGuide estimates **$0.25 to $0.50 per 20-minute session** for an electric sauna. Our [sauna running cost calculator](/sauna-running-cost-calculator/) gives a figure for your heater, routine and rate.
