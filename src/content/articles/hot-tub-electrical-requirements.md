This guide summarizes the National Electrical Code (NEC) rules for hot tubs, using local code-requirement sheets that cite the 2017 NEC and 2018 International Residential Code. It is not a substitute for your local code, your permit office or a licensed electrician. Water and electricity are a fatal combination when they are done wrong.

## The short answer

| Requirement | Rule | Code section |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GFCI protection | Required for the outlet or circuit feeding the spa, unless the spa has listed built-in GFCI | NEC 680.44 |
| Disconnect | Within sight, readily accessible, at least 5 ft from the inside wall (unless a permanent barrier) | NEC 680.12 |
| Switches | At least 5 ft from the inside wall (unless a permanent barrier) | NEC 680.22(C) |
| Receptacles | At least 6 ft from the inside wall | NEC 680.22(A) |
| Receptacles 6 to 20 ft away | Must be GFCI-protected | NEC 680.22(A)(4) |
| General-use receptacle | At least one, between 6 and 20 ft from the tub | NEC 680.22(A)(1) |
| Overhead power lines | None within 22.5 ft of the water surface | NEC 680.8(A) |
| Overhead communication lines | None within 10 ft | NEC 680.8(B) |
| Underground wiring | None under the tub or within 5 ft, except wiring that feeds it | NEC 680.10 |
| Bonding | Fixed metal parts within 5 ft of the inside wall must be bonded | NEC 680.26(B)(7) |

## GFCI protection

A ground-fault circuit interrupter shuts off power in a fraction of a second when current leaks to ground, such as through water and a person. Local code sheets based on NEC 680.44 require the outlet supplying a self-contained spa or hot tub to be GFCI-protected, unless the unit has integrated GFCI protection and is marked as such.

Receptacles **6 to 20 feet** from the tub must also be GFCI-protected under NEC 680.22(A)(4).

## The disconnect

You need a way to cut power to the tub that someone standing near it can see and reach. The code sheets we reviewed require the disconnect to be:

- **Within sight** of the tub
- **Readily accessible**
- **At least 5 feet horizontally** from the inside wall of the tub, unless a permanent barrier such as a solid fence or wall separates them

The 5-foot distance keeps a person in the water from reaching the switch. An electrical trade Q&A by Mike Holt confirms that switching devices, including circuit breakers, must be at least 5 feet from an outdoor spa or hot tub's inside walls under NEC 680.22(C), unless separated by a permanent barrier.

One note on code editions: a 2003 trade article points out that the 2002 NEC dropped the specific distance from 680.12 and required only that the disconnect be within sight. Newer local sheets include the 5-foot rule. Your area may use a different edition, so confirm with your permit office.

## Outlets, lights and fans

- **Receptacles:** at least **6 feet** from the inside wall of the tub.
- **At least one general-purpose receptacle** must be installed between **6 and 20 feet** from the tub, so nobody runs an extension cord to it.
- **Lights and ceiling fans:** none directly over the tub or within 5 feet horizontally, unless mounted at least **12 feet** above the water (NEC 680.22(B)). Fixtures between 5 and 10 feet away need GFCI protection or must be mounted at least 5 feet above the water.

## Overhead and underground wires

- **Overhead power lines:** no conductors within **22.5 feet** of the water surface (NEC 680.8(A)).
- **Overhead communication lines** such as phone or cable: none within **10 feet** (NEC 680.8(B)).
- **Underground wiring:** none under the tub or within **5 feet**, except wiring that feeds the tub (NEC 680.10). Call 811 before you dig.

## Bonding

Bonding connects nearby metal together so it all sits at the same electrical potential, which reduces shock risk. Fixed metal parts within **5 feet** of the inside wall and within **12 feet** above the water must be bonded (NEC 680.26(B)(7)). The code sheets also require that the tub's rim sit at least 28 inches above any surface within 30 inches of it, or those surfaces must be bonded too (NEC 680.42(B)).

## 120V plug-and-play vs 240V hard-wired

- **Plug-and-play spas** run from a standard 120-volt outlet. That outlet still needs GFCI protection and must meet the distance rules above. They are usually slower to heat.
- **Hard-wired spas** run on a dedicated 240-volt circuit. The breaker and wire size come from the manufacturer's installation manual, and the work typically needs a permit.

## Other code items you may hit

The same local sheets list non-electrical requirements that often come up at inspection:

- A **locking safety cover** meeting ASTM F1346
- A tub **listed to UL 1563**
- Installation that follows the **manufacturer's instructions**

## Before you buy

1. Check whether your town requires a **permit** for the tub and the circuit. Most do for a hard-wired spa.
2. Get the **installation manual** for the exact model. It lists the circuit size and any extra clearances.
3. Have a **licensed electrician** quote the run from your panel, including the disconnect and GFCI.
4. Plan the location around the **5-foot, 6-foot and 22.5-foot** distances above before you pour a pad.
