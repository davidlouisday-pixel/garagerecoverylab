This guide is based on research, not hands-on testing: we compared published price data from manufacturers and home-services cost guides. Ranges vary by region and dealer. Sources are listed at the end.

## Total cost at a glance

| Cost | Typical range | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hot tub (purchase) | $400 to $18,000+ | See tiers below |
| Electrical work | $150 to $4,200 | $150 to $500 if wiring exists; more for a subpanel or panel upgrade |
| Base or pad | $200 to $2,500+ | Gravel and plastic pads are cheapest; concrete $500 to $2,500 |
| Delivery | $200 to $500 | Often included by dealers |
| Crane (if needed) | $300 to $1,500 | When the tub must go over the house |
| Permit | $0 to $250 | Most above-ground tubs need none; check locally |
| Running cost | $30 to $100 a month | Electricity, chemicals, filters, water |

## Purchase price by tier

Prices vary by source and dealer, so treat these as ballpark figures before delivery and installation.

| Tier | Typical price | What you get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inflatable | $400 to $1,500 | Plugs into 120V, slow to heat, 2 to 5 year life |
| Entry acrylic / plug-and-play | $4,000 to $8,000 | 120V option, fewer jets, basic insulation |
| Mid-range | $8,000 to $12,000 | 240V, better insulation, more jets, better covers |
| Premium | $12,000 to $16,000 | Full-foam insulation, advanced filtration, longer warranties |
| Luxury | $16,000+ | Top brands, smart controls, custom features |

Swim spas and custom in-ground tubs start around $10,000 and can exceed $25,000.

## Installation costs

### Electrical

Plug-and-play tubs run on 120V and typically need a dedicated outlet. Everything else runs on 240V and must be hardwired by a licensed electrician. Most need a dedicated 50 or 60 amp GFCI-protected circuit with a disconnect panel near the tub.

- Connecting to existing wiring: about $150 to $500
- New circuit and wiring up to about 100 feet: about $800 to $1,600
- Full install with a subpanel: $1,900 to $4,200 is a common range, with an average around $2,300
- Main panel service upgrade if needed: about $1,400 to $2,800 more

Electricians typically charge $50 to $130 an hour, and the job usually takes 6 to 8 hours.

### Foundation

A filled tub is heavy, so it needs a flat, solid base. Typical per-square-foot prices: gravel $1 to $3, plastic spa pad $4 to $7, concrete slab $5 to $10, pavers $10 to $17, and a reinforced deck $15 to $30.

### Delivery and access

Delivery runs about $200 to $500 if not included. If the tub cannot fit through a side gate, a crane to lift it over the house costs $300 to $1,500.

## Monthly running costs

| Item | Typical monthly cost |
| --- | --- |
| Electricity | $10 to $60 |
| Chemicals | $10 to $40 |
| Water testing and filter care | $10 to $20 |
| Water refills | $2 to $10 |

Electricity is where estimates disagree most. Manufacturer ratings from the California Energy Commission put efficient tubs at about $10 to $21 a month, while home-services guides report $20 to $60. Insulation, cover quality, tub size, how hot you keep it and your winter temperatures make the difference. At about 18 cents per kWh, the U.S. average, every 100 kWh is about $18.

Larger tubs and swim spas cost more: about $80 to $120 a month for large tubs and $90 to $120 for swim spas, according to Angi.

## Maintenance and repairs to budget for

| Item | Typical cost | How often |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Filter replacement | $20 to $60 | Every 6 to 12 months |
| Pump replacement | $200 to $500 | Every 5 to 7 years |
| Heater replacement | $300 to $700 | Every 7 to 10 years |
| Control panel | $150 to $400 | As needed |
| Annual service plan | $350 to $600 a year | Optional |
| Homeowner's insurance increase | $50 to $150 a year | Ongoing |

## How to keep the total down

- **Buy for insulation, not jets.** Full-foam insulation and a thick, tight cover cut electricity more than any other feature.
- **Place it close to your panel.** Shorter wire runs cost less.
- **Check gate width before you buy** to avoid crane fees.
- **Consider plug-and-play** if you want to skip electrical work, but expect slower heating in cold weather.
- **Shop end-of-season and floor models** for dealer discounts.
