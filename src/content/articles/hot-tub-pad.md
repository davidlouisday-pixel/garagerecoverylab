This guide is based on three hot tub makers' published installation guides: Master Spas, Caldera Spas and Bullfrog Spas, listed at the end. We have not poured a hot tub pad ourselves. Your tub's own installation manual is the one that counts for its warranty, so read it before you build.

## The short answer

| Base | Works? | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reinforced concrete slab, 4 in or more | Yes, the standard | Caldera: supports at least 120 lb per sq ft |
| Pavers on compacted gravel or sand | Often | Ground must be leveled and compacted first |
| Compacted gravel base | Often | Contain it so it cannot shift |
| Prefabricated spa pad | Often | Check your maker's approval |
| Deck | Only if engineered | Consult a structural engineer or contractor |
| Grass or bare soil | No | Bullfrog: do not place a hot tub on grass |

## Why the base matters

A hot tub is heavy. Master Spas puts a typical filled tub at about **2,000 to 6,000 pounds**, and its own chart goes up to about **7,800 pounds** for its biggest model. Caldera says a filled spa can range from **2,000 to nearly 8,000 pounds**. Add the people in it.

You can estimate the water yourself: water weighs about **8.3 pounds per gallon**, so a 400-gallon tub holds about 3,300 pounds of water before you add the shell or anyone sitting in it.

If the base sinks or tilts, the shell can flex and crack, the cabinet can warp, and water sits unevenly. That is why makers are specific about the base and why some tie the warranty to it.

## Option by option

### Concrete slab (the standard)

- **Thickness:** at least **4 inches**, per all three makers. Bullfrog suggests **6 inches or more** for larger spas and swim spas.
- **Reinforcement:** rebar or wire mesh. Master Spas specifies steel reinforcement bars crossed throughout. Bullfrog notes the reinforcement should be bonded for grounding, which your electrician will handle.
- **Strength:** Caldera specifies a slab that supports at least **120 pounds per square foot**.
- **Level:** Bullfrog allows at most **half an inch of slope over 8 feet**, and says not to use shims.
- **Drainage:** Caldera asks for proper slope drainage so water runs away from the equipment.

A slab is the most work and usually the highest cost, but it is the base every maker accepts.

### Pavers

Master Spas accepts pavers on ground that is **leveled, filled with pea gravel or sand, and compacted** first. Bullfrog lists a concrete paver patio as an option on compacted soil. Use large, thick pavers and keep the whole area flat, since a single sunken paver under one corner is enough to stress the shell.

### Gravel

Caldera calls a gravel base an affordable option, and Bullfrog lists pea gravel or crushed rock. It must be compacted, leveled and edged with a frame so it cannot spread under the weight.

### Prefabricated spa pads

Caldera lists prefabricated pads as an option. These are interlocking plastic panels laid on a level, compacted base. Check that your tub's maker approves the specific pad, since approval varies by brand.

### Deck

Both Master Spas and Caldera say to have a **contractor or structural engineer** check a deck before putting a tub on it. Most decks are built for people and furniture, not several tons on a few square yards.

## Plan the space around the pad

- **Service access.** Master Spas says your dealer needs at least **3 feet of clearance** around the tub's perimeter. Caldera says walls and structures must not block access to the equipment.
- **Overhead power lines.** Master Spas says none within **15 feet**.
- **Electrical.** The disconnect location and circuit are set by code and your electrician. Our [hot tub electrical requirements](/hot-tub-electrical-requirements/) guide covers the wiring side.
- **Delivery path.** Measure gates and paths so the tub can reach the pad.
