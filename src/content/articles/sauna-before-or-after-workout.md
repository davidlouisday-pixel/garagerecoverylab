## The short answer

Go **after** you train. Every study below put the sauna after exercise, and doing it first means you start your workout already hot, sweating and with a raised heart rate.

| | Sauna before workout | Sauna after workout |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heart rate going into training | Already elevated | Normal |
| Fluid loss before you lift or run | Yes | No |
| How the studies used it | Not tested in the trials below | 15 to 30 minutes post-exercise |
| Best use | Skip it; do a light warm-up instead | Cool-down and recovery routine |

## What the research found

**Sauna plus exercise beat exercise alone (2022).** Researchers at the University of Jyväskylä in Finland ran a randomized controlled trial. Everyone trained 50 minutes, three times a week: 20 minutes of resistance work, then 30 minutes of cardio. One group added a 15-minute Finnish sauna after each session. After eight weeks, the sauna group had larger improvements in cardiorespiratory fitness and bigger drops in systolic blood pressure and total cholesterol than the exercise-only group.

**Post-run sauna improved endurance (2007).** In a small crossover study of six competitive male runners, sitting in a sauna at about 90°C (194°F) for roughly 30 minutes right after training, about 13 times over 3 weeks, increased time to exhaustion by 32%. The authors estimated that was worth about 1.9% in a race. The likely reason was a 7% increase in plasma volume. With only six runners, treat this as promising, not settled.

**Frequent sauna use and heart health (2015).** A Finnish study followed 2,315 middle-aged men for about 20 years. Compared with men who used a sauna once a week, those who went 4 to 7 times a week had a lower risk of sudden cardiac death and fatal heart disease. Sessions over 19 minutes were also linked with lower risk than sessions under 11 minutes. This shows an association, so other habits may explain part of it.

## How to do it after training

1. **Rehydrate first.** Drink water as you finish training. A short sauna can cost about a pint of sweat.
2. **Cool down for a few minutes** so your heart rate starts to settle before the heat raises it again.
3. **Stay 15 to 20 minutes.** That is the common guidance and close to what the 2022 trial used. New to saunas? Start at 5 minutes.
4. **Get out if you feel dizzy, nauseous or get a headache.** Those are dehydration warning signs.
5. **Rehydrate again afterward,** with electrolytes if you sweated heavily in both the workout and the sauna.

## Who should skip it or ask a doctor first

Cleveland Clinic advises against sauna use if you have had a recent heart attack or stroke, have been drinking alcohol, are pregnant or trying to get pregnant, or have certain heart conditions, including heart failure, severe aortic stenosis, or high or low blood pressure.
