This guide uses the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention's hot tub guidance for the water numbers. Cleaning and draining intervals vary by model, so we label those as common practice and your owner's manual has the final say. We do not own a hot tub and do not link to products yet.

## The numbers that matter

| What | Target (CDC) | If it's off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free chlorine | At least 3 ppm | Above 10 ppm, stay out until it drops |
| Bromine (if you use it instead) | 4 to 8 ppm | Above 8 ppm, wait |
| pH | 7.0 to 7.8 | Adjust before soaking |
| Water temperature | No higher than 104°F (40°C) | Turn it down |

Why it matters: warm water is a good home for germs. The CDC's hot tub pages are written because hot tubs are linked to skin rashes and to *Legionella*, the bacteria behind Legionnaires' disease. Keeping the disinfectant and pH in range is what keeps those in check.

The CDC also recommends **not using cyanuric acid** (stabilizer) or stabilized chlorine products in hot tubs. Check the label on any chlorine you buy.

## A simple routine

### Every time you soak

1. **Test chlorine (or bromine) and pH.** The CDC recommends testing at least twice a day for pools and hot tubs with regular use, and more often with many bathers. At home, test at least before each soak.
2. **Shower first.** Sweat, lotion and deodorant use up disinfectant and cloud the water.
3. **Keep it 104°F or below.**
4. **Put the cover back on** to keep debris out and heat in.

On testing kits, the CDC notes that **DPD test kits are more accurate than test strips**. Strips are fine for a quick check, but use a DPD kit when the numbers matter.

### Every week

- **Shock the water** after heavy use or a party, following the product's directions.
- **Rinse the filter** with a garden hose. Many owners do this weekly or every two weeks.
- **Wipe the waterline** to stop a scum ring from building up.
- **Check the total alkalinity** if your pH keeps drifting, since it buffers pH.

### Every month

- **Deep-clean the filter** with a filter cleaner soak, as your manual directs.
- **Clean and condition the cover**, top and underside.
- **Look over the equipment bay** for leaks or corrosion.

### Every few months

- **Drain and refill.** A common rule of thumb is every three to four months, sooner with heavy use. Your owner's manual may give a formula based on how many people use the tub.
- **Clean the shell** while it is empty, with a cleaner made for spas.
- **Replace the filter cartridge** when it no longer cleans up, often about once a year.

## Winter care

If you live somewhere cold, the biggest risk is freezing. Keep the tub running and heated rather than turning it off, keep the cover locked down, and check it after any power outage. If you will not use it for months, drain and winterize it fully, following the manual, or have a dealer do it.
