This guide is based on published cost data and manufacturer and retailer pages, checked on November 27, 2026. Each source is labeled as a cost site, manufacturer or retailer, because those have different incentives. We have not bought or tested these chairs.

## The short answer

| Tier | Typical price | What you usually get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entry | About $800 to $1,500 | Basic rollers, airbags, fewer programs |
| Mid-range | About $1,500 to $5,000 | Longer roller track, zero gravity, foot rollers, heat |
| Premium | About $5,000 to $12,000+ | More advanced rollers, body scanning, more programs and build quality |

Tier ranges are from LatestCost. The site puts a typical chair at about **$3,500**, and a typical delivered total at about **$3,900**.

Other sources land close to this:

- **Human Touch (manufacturer):** a good chair costs **$2,000 to $8,000+**. It calls **$2,000 to $4,000** the zone with real engineering improvements and **$5,000 to $8,000+** premium.
- **Furniture For Life (retailer):** luxury chairs at about **$6,000 to $10,000**, with low-cost chairs in the low thousands.

## What you pay for

### 1. The chair

Price mostly tracks the massage mechanism and how much of your body it covers. Things that tend to push the price up:

- **Roller track length and shape.** Longer tracks that curve under the seat reach your glutes and hamstrings.
- **Zero gravity recline.** Now common in mid-range chairs. See [zero gravity massage chairs explained](/zero-gravity-massage-chair/).
- **Foot and calf rollers, heat and stretching programs.**
- **Body scanning** to fit rollers to your height and spine shape.
- **Build quality and warranty length.**

### 2. Delivery and setup

LatestCost's figures:

| Item | Typical cost |
| --- | --- |
| Standard delivery | $0 to $250 |
| White-glove delivery to your room, with setup | $150 to $600 |
| Removing an old chair | $50 to $200 |

Chairs weigh roughly **150 to 300 pounds**. Many retailers offer free delivery on some models, so check before you add it to the budget.

### 3. Extended protection

LatestCost puts extended protection plans at about **$100 to $800**. Read the manufacturer warranty first. What matters is how long parts, labor and in-home service are covered, not just the headline number of years.

### 4. Returns

Return terms vary a lot. As one example, Furniture For Life offers a 90-day in-home trial, but a return is refunded minus shipping and a **15% restocking fee**. On a $4,000 chair, that fee alone is $600. Read the return policy before you order.

## Repair costs

From LatestCost's repair guide:

| Repair | Typical cost |
| --- | --- |
| Diagnostic visit | $75 to $200 |
| Minor repair | $100 to $450 |
| Roller or massage motor | $300 to $900 |
| Control board or display | $350 to $1,100 |
| Upholstery panel | $150 to $900 |
| Pickup and return of the chair | $100 to $400 |

Most repairs fall between **$100 and $700**, and major jobs can pass **$1,000**. A strong warranty with in-home labor can be worth more than a small discount.

## Running costs

Massage chairs use little power. Furniture For Life says running costs are typically **less than a few dollars a month**. A chair only draws real power while a program runs.

## How to spend less

1. **Shop sales.** LatestCost says promotions of **10 to 25%** off are common.
2. **Consider floor or open-box models,** which LatestCost says sell for **10 to 30%** off. Check the warranty still applies in full.
3. **Pay for what you will use.** If you only want back and neck massage, you may not need premium foot rollers and stretching programs.
4. **Compare total cost,** not sticker price: delivery, warranty and return terms change the real number.

## Is a massage chair worth it?

That depends on how often you will use it and what you expect from it. A 2025 trial found 15-minute sessions three times a week eased stress and neck and back pain in hospital workers, which is a modest but real benefit.
