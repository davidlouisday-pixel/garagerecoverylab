## The short answer

| Option | Thickness | Good for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rubber stall mats (4x6 ft) | 3/4 in | Best value for lifting | About 100 lb each, strong smell at first |
| Interlocking rubber tiles | About 3/4 to 1 in | Neater look, easier to move | Cost more per square foot |
| Rubber rolls | About 3/4 to 7/8 in | Large areas with few seams | Heavy, harder to cut and fit |
| Foam tiles | Often 3/4 in | Stretching, bodyweight work | Not suitable for free weights |

## Why rubber over bare concrete

A garage slab is hard on dropped weights, hard on the weights themselves, and loud. Rubber spreads the impact, reduces noise and gives a grippy surface. Garage Gym Reviews rates 3/4-inch stall mats well for **noise reduction from dropped barbells** and their anti-skid surface. Greatmats notes that rubber also cushions compared with bare concrete.

## Option 1: horse stall mats

This is what most garage gyms use, because it is the cheapest way to get thick rubber.

From the listings we checked:

- **Size:** 4 by 6 feet, **24 square feet** per mat
- **Thickness:** 3/4 inch
- **Weight:** about **100 pounds** each
- **Price:** under **$60** per mat at Tractor Supply, per Garage Gym Reviews, and **$64.73** per mat for a comparable Greatmats mat (about $2.50 to $2.70 per square foot)
- **Material:** recycled, vulcanized rubber

The downsides are real:

- **Weight.** At 100 pounds each, plan for two people or a hand truck.
- **Smell.** Greatmats says its mats "do have a high rubber odor" and should be used in a well-ventilated area. The smell fades; one buyer said it lasted about a week.
- **Texture.** Garage Gym Reviews notes the raised surface pattern is harder to clean than a smooth floor.

## Option 2: interlocking rubber tiles

Tiles such as 2 by 2 foot interlocking rubber squares are lighter to carry, fit together without gaps and look more finished. Greatmats lists 1-inch interlocking weight tiles and 7/8-inch tiles rated for impact. They cost more per square foot than stall mats, which matters over a full bay.

## Option 3: rubber rolls

Rolls come about 4 feet wide and can run the length of a room, which means fewer seams. Greatmats lists a 22.5 mm (about 7/8 inch) roll. They are heavy and harder to trim neatly, so they suit people covering a large, simple rectangle.

## Not recommended for lifting: foam tiles

Foam puzzle tiles are fine for stretching or bodyweight work, but Greatmats says foam "cannot" be used with free weights. Dropped plates dent and tear it.

## How many mats do you need?

| Area | Square feet | 4x6 stall mats (24 sq ft each) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One-wall setup, 8 x 12 ft | 96 | 4 |
| Half a bay, 10 x 20 ft | 200 | 9 |
| Two-car bay, 20 x 20 ft | 400 | 17 |

Add one or two mats for cuts and edges. These counts are our arithmetic from the 24 square feet per mat.

## How to lay stall mats

1. **Air them out first.** A few days in the driveway or an open garage takes the edge off the smell.
2. **Clean and dry the slab.** Greatmats says to dry-lay on a flat, dry subfloor.
3. **Start in a corner** against the straightest wall and work out.
4. **Butt the mats tightly together.** Their weight keeps them from sliding.
5. **Cut with a utility knife.** Greatmats says to make repeated scores with a sharp utility knife. Use a straightedge such as a drywall square, and score several times rather than forcing one cut.
6. **Leave a drain path** if your garage floor slopes to a drain.

## Add a deadlift platform if you pull heavy

Mats alone take a lot of punishment from repeated heavy deadlifts. Many lifters build a platform on top of the mats where they pull, with a hard center to stand on and rubber where the plates land. If you only lift moderate weights, good mats may be enough.

## Keep flooring away from water

If your garage also holds a cold plunge or sauna, keep gym mats out of the wet zone or use drainable mats there. Our [garage gym ideas](/garage-gym-ideas/) guide covers layouts, and the [garage sauna](/garage-sauna/) guide covers building a sauna next to the gym.
