This guide is based on published studies, listed at the end. Cole uses the sauna at his gym four or more times a week after training, and his routine is labeled separately below.

## The short answer

| Sessions per week | What the Finnish research linked it with, compared with once a week |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | The comparison group |
| 2 to 3 | Modestly lower risk of some outcomes, such as respiratory illness and pneumonia |
| 4 to 7 | The largest reductions: sudden cardiac death, dementia, stroke, high blood pressure |

If you are starting out, one or two sessions a week is plenty. If you are already comfortable, 3 to 4 a week gets you into the range where the research found the strongest associations.

## Where these numbers come from

Almost all of the frequency data comes from one long-running project: the **Kuopio Ischemic Heart Disease Risk Factor study** in eastern Finland. Researchers recorded how often middle-aged men used a sauna, then followed them for 20 years or more. In the main paper, the average participant went about **2 times a week** for about **14 minutes**, at an average of about **174°F**.

Here is what the papers found when they compared frequent users with once-a-week users.

### Heart: sudden cardiac death

In **2,315 men** followed for a median of **20.7 years**, 4 to 7 sessions a week was linked to about a **63% lower risk of sudden cardiac death** than one session a week (*JAMA Internal Medicine*, 2015). Fatal heart disease and death from any cause followed the same pattern.

### Brain: dementia

In the same men, 4 to 7 sessions a week was linked to a **66% lower risk of dementia** and a **65% lower risk of Alzheimer's disease** (*Age and Ageing*, 2017).

### Blood pressure

In **1,621 men** without high blood pressure at the start, followed for a median of **24.7 years**:

- **4 to 7 sessions a week** was linked to about **46% lower risk** of developing high blood pressure (hazard ratio 0.54), and the link held after further adjustment.
- **2 to 3 sessions a week** showed a smaller reduction that was **not statistically clear** (hazard ratio 0.76, with a confidence interval that crossed 1).

That second point matters. In this study, the benefit only clearly showed up in the most frequent group (*American Journal of Hypertension*, 2017).

### Lungs: respiratory illness and pneumonia

In **1,935 men** followed for a median of **25.6 years**, compared with once a week or less:

- **2 to 3 sessions a week** was linked to about **27% fewer** hospital-diagnosed respiratory illnesses such as COPD, asthma and pneumonia.
- **4 or more** sessions a week was linked to about **41% fewer**.

A separate analysis of **2,210 men** found about **31% lower pneumonia risk** at 2 to 3 sessions and **44% lower** at 4 or more, after adjusting for major risk factors.

### Stroke

In **1,628 men and women** aged 53 to 74 followed for 15 years, 4 to 7 sessions a week was linked to people being **61% less likely to have a stroke** than once-a-week users, according to the University of Eastern Finland. This is one of the few results in the series that included women.

## What this does not prove

These are observational studies. People who can sauna most days may be healthier, wealthier or less busy in ways the researchers could not fully adjust for. The studies were also mostly middle-aged Finnish men using traditional saunas, so the results may not apply in the same way to women, younger people or infrared saunas.

The honest summary: more frequent sauna use is consistently linked with better outcomes, and the pattern repeats across heart, brain and lung results. Nobody has run a long trial that randomly assigned people to 1 or 5 sessions a week.

## Cole's routine

Cole uses his gym's electric sauna **four or more times a week**, for about **20 minutes** at **180 to 190°F**, right after training. That puts him in the most frequent group in the Finnish studies, with longer and hotter sessions than their average. He says it leaves him looser and more clear-minded, and helps him sleep. That is one person's experience, not evidence.

## How to build up

1. **Weeks 1 to 2:** one or two sessions a week, 5 to 10 minutes, at the cooler end of the range.
2. **Weeks 3 to 4:** add a third session before you add time or heat.
3. **After that:** settle into what you can keep up. For most people that is 2 to 4 sessions a week of 15 to 20 minutes. See [how long to stay in a sauna](/how-long-should-you-stay-in-a-sauna/) and [how hot a sauna should be](/how-hot-should-a-sauna-be/).

If you use it after lifting, our guides on [sauna before or after a workout](/sauna-before-or-after-workout/) and [sauna for muscle recovery](/sauna-for-muscle-recovery/) cover the timing research.

## When to skip a session

The 2018 Mayo Clinic Proceedings review calls sauna bathing safe for most people when used sensibly, but lists reasons to avoid it or check with a doctor first: unstable angina, a recent heart attack, severe aortic stenosis, uncontrolled high blood pressure, decompensated heart failure, and fever or acute infection. It also warns against combining sauna and alcohol. Frequency does not change those rules.
