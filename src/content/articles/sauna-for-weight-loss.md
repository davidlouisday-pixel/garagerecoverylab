This guide is based on the studies listed at the end. Cole uses his gym's sauna four or more times a week, and has not used it for weight loss, so there is no first-hand section here.

## The short answer

- **The scale drops** after a sauna, usually by around a pound.
- **That weight is sweat.** It comes back when you drink.
- **No good study shows** a sauna alone causes meaningful fat loss.
- **The real risk** of using a sauna to lose weight is dehydration.

## What the research measured

### How much weight a session takes off

The largest study we found is by Podstawski and colleagues, published in 2014. They weighed **674 sedentary university students** (326 women and 348 men, aged 19 to 20) before and after a dry sauna at **194°F (90°C)** and 35% humidity: two **10-minute** sessions with a 5-minute break.

| Group | Average weight lost | Highest single loss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Women | 0.37 kg (about 0.8 lb) | 1.2 kg (about 2.6 lb) |
| Men | 0.50 kg (about 1.1 lb) | 1.3 kg (about 2.9 lb) |

Heavier students lost more. Men with a BMI over 25 lost about **0.82 kg (1.8 lb)** on average. The authors' takeaway was not "saunas help you lose weight". It was that people with a higher BMI are at **higher risk of dehydration**.

### Where the weight goes

A 2022 study by the same lead researcher put **30 active men** through four **12-minute** Finnish sauna rounds at 194°F, with cold water dips in between. They lost weight again, and the authors concluded the loss was related to **fluid lost through sweating**.

That matches basic physiology. A sauna makes you sweat heavily, and sweat is water and salt. Drink a liter of water and roughly a kilogram comes back.

### What about calories and fat?

Your heart rate rises in a sauna and your body works to cool itself, so you do use some extra energy. But we did not find a quality study showing that sauna sessions on their own lead to meaningful fat loss over time.

A 2018 systematic review of 40 dry sauna studies included one small far-infrared study in people with obesity and other heart disease risk factors that reported benefits. One small study is not enough to recommend a sauna as a weight-loss tool.

## How a sauna can still help

The honest pitch is indirect:

- **Recovery.** Some small trials found post-workout sauna sessions improved recovery and reduced soreness. If that helps you train more consistently, that matters more than the sweat. See [sauna for muscle recovery](/sauna-for-muscle-recovery/).
- **Blood pressure and heart health.** Long-term Finnish studies linked frequent sauna use with lower risk of high blood pressure and fatal heart disease. Those are associations, not proof, and they are not about weight. See [how often should you use a sauna](/how-often-should-you-use-a-sauna/).

## Do not use a sauna to cut weight

Cleveland Clinic lists dehydration as one of the biggest sauna risks, with signs such as thirst, headache, muscle cramps and confusion. Sitting in a sauna to drop weight fast, especially while also restricting water, stacks those risks.

- **Drink water** before and after every session.
- **Keep sessions moderate:** 15 to 20 minutes for most people. See [how long to stay in a sauna](/how-long-should-you-stay-in-a-sauna/).
- **Skip alcohol** before or during a sauna.
- **Get out** if you feel dizzy, sick or get a headache.
