This guide is based on published studies. Cole uses the sauna at his gym after training, four or more times a week, and his experience is labeled separately below.

## The short answer

A sauna after training is likely to make you feel less sore and may help explosive strength bounce back faster. The evidence comes from small trials, mostly with athletes, so the effect is real but modest.

| What was measured | What the studies found |
| --- | --- |
| Muscle soreness the next day | Lower after a post-workout infrared sauna (2023) |
| Jump power the next day | Dropped less after sauna than after resting (2023) |
| Muscle growth over 6 weeks | No difference with or without sauna (2025) |
| Jump power over 6 weeks | Improved more in the sauna group (2025) |
| Soreness from heat right after exercise | Reduced, and more than heat applied a day later (2017) |

## What the research found

**One sauna session after strength training (2023).** Sixteen male basketball players did a hard strength and plyometric workout twice, a week apart. After one workout they rested for 20 minutes; after the other they sat in an infrared sauna at about 109°F (43°C) for 20 minutes. Fourteen hours later, jump performance had dropped less after the sauna session, muscle soreness was lower, and the players rated their recovery higher. The sauna did not disrupt their heart rate or heart rate variability overnight.

**Six weeks of sauna after training (2025).** Forty female team-sport athletes did six weeks of strength and power training. Half used an infrared sauna for 10 minutes at about 122°F (50°C) after training, three times a week. Both groups built the same amount of muscle, but the sauna group's jump height and peak power improved more.

**Heat right after exercise (2017).** In a study of 60 adults who did hard squat sets to make their legs sore, the group that wore low-level heat wraps for 8 hours right after exercise had less soreness than the group that got no heat. Heat applied 24 hours later helped less. This used heat wraps, not a sauna, but it points to the same idea: warm the muscles soon after training.

**Endurance and blood volume (2007).** In a small study of six runners, about 30 minutes in a sauna after training, roughly 13 times over three weeks, raised plasma volume by about 7% and time to exhaustion by 32%. More plasma volume is one likely reason heat helps endurance.

## Why heat may help

The studies point to a few likely reasons, though none of them proves the mechanism:

- **More blood flow** to working muscles, which may help clear waste and deliver nutrients.
- **Looser tissue.** Heat increases flexibility, which matches the "less stiff" feeling many people report.
- **Relaxation.** Players in the 2023 trial rated their recovery higher, and feeling recovered affects how you train next.

## Cole's experience

Cole trains seven days a week and sits in his gym's electric sauna after training, four or more times a week, for about 20 minutes at 180 to 190°F. That is a traditional sauna, much hotter than the infrared saunas in the studies above.

In his words, he comes out feeling "loose and less stiff," "clear minded," and finds sleep and recovery easier. That is one person's experience, not a study result, but it lines up with what the 2023 trial measured: less soreness and better perceived recovery.

## How to use a sauna for recovery

1. **Go right after training.** That is how the studies used it.
2. **Stay 15 to 20 minutes.** The main recovery trial used 20 minutes; new users should start with 5 to 10. See [how long to stay in a sauna](/how-long-should-you-stay-in-a-sauna/).
3. **Infrared or traditional both work.** The studies used infrared at 109 to 122°F. Traditional saunas run hotter, so shorten your session if you feel dizzy.
4. **Drink water before and after.** Training plus sauna doubles your sweat loss.
5. **Keep the basics first.** Sleep, protein and sensible training load matter more than any recovery tool.

If you train at home, the deciding factors for a home sauna are wiring, heat and running cost. Our [best home saunas](/best-home-sauna/) guide compares three on exactly that, and [sauna before or after a workout](/sauna-before-or-after-workout/) covers timing.

## Who should be careful

Talk to a doctor first if you have a heart condition, high or low blood pressure, or are pregnant, and skip the sauna after drinking alcohol. Cleveland Clinic lists these among the reasons to avoid sauna use.
