This guide is based on published research and medical guidance. Cole uses an electric sauna four or more times a week, but the numbers below come from the sources listed at the end.

## Quick guide

| | Beginner | Regular user |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time per session | 5 to 10 minutes | 15 to 20 minutes |
| Traditional sauna temperature | Lower end, about 150 to 160°F | 150 to 195°F |
| Infrared sauna temperature | About 110 to 120°F | 110 to 150°F |
| How often | 1 to 2 times a week while you adjust | Several times a week |

## How long: 15 to 20 minutes

Cleveland Clinic's guidance is to keep a session around **15 to 20 minutes**, and to start at **5 minutes** if you are new and add time gradually. That lines up with the research:

- A 2022 randomized trial in Finland added **15 minutes** of sauna after exercise and saw bigger drops in blood pressure and cholesterol than exercise alone.
- In a 20-year Finnish study, sessions **longer than 19 minutes** were linked with lower risk of fatal heart disease than sessions under 11 minutes. Again, this is an association.

Longer is not automatically better. Get out if you feel dizzy, nauseous, or get a headache.

## How hot: it depends on the type

**Traditional saunas** heat the air, typically to **150 to 195°F**, with low humidity unless you pour water on the stones. **Infrared saunas** warm your body directly, so the air only needs to reach about **110 to 150°F**. If you are comparing the two, our [infrared vs traditional sauna](/infrared-vs-traditional-sauna/) guide covers heat-up time, wiring and running cost.

## How often: the case for several times a week

The best-known data comes from a Finnish study of **2,315 middle-aged men** followed for a median of **20.7 years**:

| Sessions per week | Share who died of any cause during follow-up |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | 49.1% |
| 2 to 3 | 37.8% |
| 4 to 7 | 30.8% |

After adjusting for other risk factors, men who used a sauna 4 to 7 times a week still had a lower risk of sudden cardiac death than once-a-week users. Sauna users may differ in other ways, such as income or overall health, so this does not prove the sauna caused the difference.

## Staying safe

1. **Hydrate before and after.** Harvard Health notes that a short sauna session can cost about a pint of sweat.
2. **Skip alcohol** before and during a session.
3. **Ask a doctor first** if you have had a recent heart attack or stroke, have heart failure, severe aortic stenosis, or high or low blood pressure, or are pregnant or trying to get pregnant.
4. **Train first, sauna second.** If you combine it with a workout, go afterward.
