This guide combines Finnish tourism and sauna guidance with the health sources listed at the end. Cole uses a traditional electric sauna with rocks at his gym, so the steps below are written for that kind of sauna.

## The short version

| Step | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Before | Drink some water, skip alcohol, shower |
| 2. First round | Sit on a towel for 10 to 15 minutes, or less as a beginner |
| 3. Cool off | Cool shower, fresh air or a cold dip |
| 4. Rest | Sit for a few minutes and drink water |
| 5. Repeat | Another round or two if you feel good |
| 6. Finish | Shower, rehydrate, eat something light |

## Before you go in

- **Drink water.** You will sweat a lot. Cleveland Clinic lists dehydration as one of the main sauna risks.
- **No alcohol.** The 2018 Mayo Clinic Proceedings review by the Finnish sauna research group warns that alcohol plus sauna raises the risk of low blood pressure, heart rhythm problems and falls.
- **Don't go in on a full stomach** or when you are ill. The same review lists fever and acute illness as reasons to skip it.
- **Shower.** Visit Jyväskylä, Visit Tampere and finland.fi all put this first. A warm shower also helps you start sweating sooner.
- **Take off jewelry and glasses.** Metal gets hot.

## In the sauna

1. **Sit on a towel.** Visit Jyväskylä says a seat towel is always needed. It is also more comfortable on hot wood.
2. **Pick your height.** Heat rises, so the top bench is hottest. In a 2026 study from the University of Jyväskylä, air averaged about **51°C (124°F) at sitting height** and **69°C (156°F) at head height**. Start on a lower bench.
3. **Stay 10 to 15 minutes.** That is the round length Visit Jyväskylä describes. Columbia University's health service gives **5 to 20 minutes** for a traditional sauna. Beginners should start at the short end.
4. **Add steam if you like.** In a traditional sauna you can ladle a little water onto the hot stones to make steam, called löyly in Finnish. It makes the room feel much hotter. In a shared sauna, ask the others first.
5. **Get out if you feel unwell.** Dizziness, nausea or a pounding headache mean it is time to leave, whatever the clock says.

Our guide to [how hot a sauna should be](/how-hot-should-a-sauna-be/) covers temperatures, and [how long to stay in a sauna](/how-long-should-you-stay-in-a-sauna/) goes deeper on timing.

## Cool off and rest

Finns cool down between rounds with a cool shower, fresh air or a dip in a lake. Then they sit for a few minutes, drink water and go back in. Stand up slowly when you leave the sauna, because heat widens blood vessels and you can feel light-headed.

A cold plunge between rounds is common, but it is not for everyone. The 2018 Mayo Clinic Proceedings review says jumping into cold water right after a sauna is not usually recommended for people with heart disease.

## How many rounds?

One round is fine. Many people do two or three, with a rest between. Visit Tampere says to repeat as often as you like, which is a fair rule for healthy adults who feel good. Start with one or two and see how you feel the next day.

## After the sauna

- **Shower** to rinse off sweat. Visit Jyväskylä suggests a warm shower after the last round.
- **Rehydrate** with water.
- **Eat something light** if you are hungry.

## Gym or home sauna?

In a gym, follow the posted rules, which often cover clothing, towels and water on the stones. At home you set the rules, but the same safety points apply. If you train first, our guide to [sauna before or after a workout](/sauna-before-or-after-workout/) covers timing.

## Who should check with a doctor first

The Mayo Clinic Proceedings review says sauna use is safe for most people when done sensibly. It lists these as reasons to avoid it or see a doctor first:

- Unstable angina, a recent heart attack or severe aortic stenosis
- Uncontrolled high blood pressure or decompensated heart failure
- Fever or acute illness

Cleveland Clinic adds that you should talk to a doctor first if you are pregnant.

## Cole's routine

Cole uses his gym's sauna **four or more times a week**, right after training. He stays about **20 minutes at 180 to 190°F**. That is longer and hotter than where a beginner should start. Work up to it over weeks, not days.
