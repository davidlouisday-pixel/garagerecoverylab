This guide is based on published cost data from three home-services cost guides and list prices from retailer pages, checked on November 27, 2026. We have not bought these saunas. Prices change often, and saunas go on sale frequently, so treat model prices as a guide.

## The short answer

| Sauna type | Typical cost | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Portable or sauna blanket | $200 to $2,000 | HomeGuide |
| Infrared, installed | $2,300 to $8,500 | HomeGuide. Many plug into a standard outlet. |
| Indoor prefab room, installed | $2,300 to $10,500 | HomeGuide |
| Outdoor prefab or barrel, installed | $5,500 to $14,500 | HomeGuide |
| Pre-cut custom kit, installed | $4,300 to $11,500 | HomeGuide |
| Fully custom sauna | $8,000 to $34,000 | HomeGuide |

Across all types, HomeGuide puts the typical installed cost at **$3,000 to $10,000**, Angi at **$2,500 to $7,000** for most home saunas, and Fixr at **$3,000 to $6,000**, with a national average of **$4,500**.

## What you pay for

### 1. The sauna itself

Real list prices give a better feel than ranges. These are regular (non-sale) prices we checked for popular outdoor barrel and cabin saunas:

| Model | Size | Heater | List price |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Almost Heaven Salem | 2 person barrel | Electric | $4,485 |
| Almost Heaven Watoga | 2 to 4 person barrel | Harvia 6 kW electric | $5,184 |
| Dundalk Canadian Timber Serenity | 3 to 4 person barrel | Not included (7.5 kW recommended) | $6,158 |
| Dundalk Canadian Timber Tranquility | 4 to 5 person barrel | Not included (7.5 kW recommended) | $6,514 |
| SaunaLife G4 | Up to 6 person cabin kit | Not listed | $8,640 |

Two things change the real price a lot:

- **Sales.** When we checked, both retailers selling Almost Heaven had Black Friday discounts of 10 to 30%. The Watoga was $4,665 instead of $5,184.
- **What is included.** Some saunas come with a heater, and some do not. Shipping can add hundreds of dollars. The Watoga listing added $699 for freight.

### 2. The heater

If your sauna does not include one, HomeGuide prices heaters alone at:

- **Electric:** $700 to $3,800
- **Wood-burning:** $800 to $3,000
- **Infrared emitters:** $200 to $600

Our [sauna heater size calculator](/sauna-heater-size-calculator/) shows what power you need for your room.

### 3. Electrical work

A traditional electric heater typically needs a **dedicated 240V circuit**, and the Watoga, for example, lists a 240V, 30 amp hardwired connection.

- HomeGuide: **$250 to $900** for a dedicated circuit
- Fixr: **$500 to $800**
- Angi: **$300 to $800** for electrical work during installation

Costs rise if your panel is full or the sauna sits far from the house. Many infrared saunas plug into a standard 120V outlet instead, which can skip this cost. Our [infrared vs traditional sauna](/infrared-vs-traditional-sauna/) guide explains the difference.

### 4. Assembly and installation

- **Prefab kit, professional assembly:** $300 to $1,500 (HomeGuide, Angi)
- **Pre-cut kit, professional assembly:** $1,500 to $2,500 (HomeGuide, Angi)
- **Fully custom:** labor is often 30 to 50% of the total, per Angi

Many barrel saunas are sold as DIY kits. One retailer offers optional professional installation of Dundalk barrels for **$800 to $3,000**, paid to the installer.

### 5. Site prep and extras

An outdoor sauna needs a level, solid base. Budget for a pad or deck if you do not have one. Angi lists a ventilation fan at **$250 to $600** where one is needed, and a permit is likely for outdoor saunas and new circuits.

## What a sauna costs to run

- **HomeGuide:** about **$0.25 to $0.50 per 20-minute session** for an electric sauna, and $4 to $8 per session for wood.
- **Fixr:** about **$15 to $20 a month** for infrared, $25 to $30 for electric, and $20 to $40 for wood.

Your actual cost depends on heater size, preheat time, how often you go and your electricity rate. Our [sauna running cost calculator](/sauna-running-cost-calculator/) works it out for your setup.

## Three example budgets

These combine the figures above. They are estimates, not quotes.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

## How to spend less

1. **Buy during a sale.** Discounts of 10 to 30% were common when we checked.
2. **Go infrared** if you do not need traditional heat, to skip the 240V circuit.
3. **Put it close to your panel.** Shorter wire runs cost less.
4. **Assemble a kit yourself,** but hire a licensed electrician for the heater connection.
