## What the research says about massage

### The Cochrane review

In 2015, Furlan and colleagues updated the Cochrane review of massage for low back pain. Their conclusions:

- They had **very little confidence** that massage is an effective treatment for low back pain.
- For acute, subacute and chronic low back pain, pain improved with massage **only in the short term**.
- People with subacute and chronic pain also had **better function** than with inactive treatments, again only short term.
- There were **no serious adverse events**. The most common side effect was **increased pain**, reported by **1.5 to 25 percent** of participants.

### The American College of Physicians guideline

The ACP's 2017 guideline for low back pain says most people with acute or subacute pain improve over time whatever they do. For those patients, it recommends starting with **non-drug options: superficial heat** (moderate-quality evidence) **or massage, acupuncture or spinal manipulation** (low-quality evidence). For chronic pain it puts the emphasis on exercise, rehabilitation and similar active approaches.

### What this means for a chair

Both of these are about **hands-on massage by a person**, not machines. A chair cannot adjust to you the way a therapist can. So the honest reading is:

- A massage chair may give **short-term comfort** for everyday back aches.
- **Heat** is one of the best-supported comfort options in the guideline, and many chairs include lumbar heat.
- A chair is **not a substitute for exercise** or for a doctor's assessment of serious pain.

## Features that matter for back pain

| Feature | Why it helps |
| --- | --- |
| **Lumbar heat** | Heat is a guideline-backed comfort option for back pain |
| **Adjustable intensity** | Lets you start gentle, since increased pain is the most common side effect of massage |
| **Body scan** | Lines the rollers up with your spine and shoulders |
| **L-track** | Extends the rollers down under the seat, reaching the lower back and glutes |
| **Zero-gravity recline** | Spreads your weight and takes some load off the spine; see [zero-gravity massage chairs](/zero-gravity-massage-chair/) |
| **Spot or partial programs** | Lets you focus on the lower back instead of a full-body cycle |

## How to use one with a sore back

1. **Start on the lowest intensity** for the first week.
2. **Keep sessions short,** 10 to 15 minutes, and see how your back feels the next morning.
3. **Use heat** if the chair has it.
4. **Skip rollers directly on a fresh injury.** Let a new strain settle for a few days first.
5. **Keep moving.** The ACP guideline puts exercise first for chronic back pain.
6. **Stop if pain becomes sharp, spreads down a leg or comes with numbness.**

## When to see a doctor first

Do not rely on a chair if your back pain:

- started after a fall or injury
- comes with **numbness, tingling or weakness** in your legs
- comes with **trouble controlling your bladder or bowels**
- comes with **fever** or **unexplained weight loss**
- is getting **worse** instead of better over a few weeks
