## 1. Canuck Cold tub + chiller: best overall

Canuck Cold's chillers hold anywhere from **37 to 104°F**, so the same setup works as a cold plunge in summer and a hot soak in winter. Water runs through a filter with **automatic ozone sanitization every 15 minutes**. The chillers are cETLus, cTUVus or CE certified, which matters more than it sounds: Canuck points out that a lot of cold plunge equipment sold online is not certified to a recognized testing lab.

It is modular, so you can buy a tub, a chiller or a bundle, and replace one part without replacing the other. The chiller is IPX4 rated for indoor and covered outdoor use.

**Price:** about $5,999 for a tub and chiller bundle, checked November 24, 2026.

**Skip it if:** warranty length is your top concern. Canuck lists 1 year.

## 2. Plunge All-In Gen 2: best all-in-one

The All-In puts the chiller inside the tub's cabinet, so there is no separate box and hose next to it. It cools to **37°F**, uses ozone plus two-stage filtration, and you set the temperature and get filter reminders from the Plunge app. Setup is plug in and fill, with no permanent plumbing, but it needs a dedicated circuit.

The tub is long, about 77 x 32 inches, and you lie back full length. Heating to 104°F is a $500 add-on.

**Price:** $6,990 on sale (list $9,990), checked November 25, 2026.

**Skip it if:** you are paying list price. At $9,990 the Canuck bundle does more for less.

## 3. Ice Barrel 300: best budget

If you are not sure you will plunge every day, start here. The Ice Barrel 300 is an upright, insulated tub (R16) made in the USA, with a limited lifetime warranty. There is no chiller, so you add ice, and water care is up to you.

It weighs 61 lb empty and about 700 lb filled, and it sits upright, so it takes far less floor space than a reclined tub. Ice Barrel sells a chiller for $4,299 if you upgrade later.

**Price:** $1,449, checked November 25, 2026.

**Skip it if:** you want cold water on demand, or you are 6 feet or taller. Ice Barrel lists the 300's max height as 6 ft 0 in; the 500 goes to 6 ft 9 in.

## Chiller or ice: what to know before you buy

**How often you will plunge decides it.** Ice is cheap to start but a chore to repeat. A chiller costs thousands more but makes a daily plunge take two minutes of setup instead of twenty.

**Power and placement.** Chillers need a GFCI-protected circuit and airflow around the unit. A garage is ideal, because it handles splashes and the weight of a filled tub.

**Certification.** Look for an NRTL mark such as ETL, UL or TUV on any chiller. It means an independent lab tested the unit for electrical safety around water.

**Health.** Cold water raises heart rate and blood pressure fast. Talk to a doctor first if you have a heart condition.

## Bottom line

Buy the **Canuck Cold tub + chiller** for the most capable setup per dollar. Choose the **Plunge All-In** if you want everything in one cabinet and catch it on sale, and the **Ice Barrel 300** if you want to try daily plunging before spending $5,000 or more.
